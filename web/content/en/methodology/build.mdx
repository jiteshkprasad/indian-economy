**Step one: write down what the sources say.** Nine building blocks (production, factor incomes, households, taxes, government, financial firms, the rest of the world, capital and finance, and the closing accounts) wrote <DataFig path="sam.entries" fmt="count"/> entries into <DataFig path="sam.cells_nonzero" fmt="count"/> cells, using <DataFig path="sam.refs" fmt="count"/> kinds of flow. Each entry carries a source record and one of three statuses: **official** (a published figure used as it is), **derived** (arithmetic on published figures) or **estimated** (a key, a structure or a model applied to a published total).

**Step two: write down what must hold.** There are <DataFig path="methodology.constraints" fmt="count"/> constraints. Most are published totals that the map must reproduce, each with a tolerance: GDP, consumption, investment, exports and imports to within a crore or two; some taxes within a few per cent of cash collections, because the national accounts record taxes when they are due, not when the cash arrives. A few are floors, caps or **judgement bands**: ranges we chose where no source pins a figure.

**Step three: balance.** The first assembly did not balance. Some accounts received more than they paid out, mostly because the new national-accounts series does not yet publish who pays interest, dividends and transfers to whom, so older structures and survey keys had to fill those flows. Balancing finds the **smallest set of proportional changes** that makes every account balance while keeping every constraint (a cross-entropy method). Each entry has a role:

- **fixed** entries never move;
- **anchored** entries are official or derived figures that may move only inside a small box around the published number;
- **free** entries are estimates; the less confident the estimate, the more freely it may move.

The solver finished in <DataFig path="sam.balancing.iterations" fmt="count"/> iterations, and its answer comes with a mathematical certificate that, by that measure, no smaller set of changes keeps every constraint. Every account's receipts now equal its payments.

**How much did balancing move?** In all, <DataFig path="sam.balancing.moved_abs" fmt="inr"/> (the sum of every change, ignoring sign). That is <DataFig path="methodology.moved_abs_gdp"/>, and <DataFig path="sam.balancing.moved_share_of_abs" fmt="pct"/> of the absolute value of the whole SAM. Almost all of it fell on estimated, freely adjustable entries; no fixed entry moved.

**Large moves are findings, not noise.** Every move of at least <DataFig path="methodology.explain_abs"/>, or of at least <DataFig path="methodology.explain_small_abs"/> and <DataFig path="methodology.explain_small_rel"/> of the entry, needs a written explanation with a verdict. <DataFig path="methodology.explain_entries"/> entries and <DataFig path="methodology.explain_cells"/> cells needed one. For entries, <DataFig path="methodology.explain_entries_explained"/> are **explained**, <DataFig path="methodology.explain_entries_level_unexplained"/> are explained in direction but **not in level**, and <DataFig path="methodology.explain_entries_unexplained"/> are **unexplained**: balancing moved them and no source says why. For cells the figures are <DataFig path="methodology.explain_cells_explained"/>, <DataFig path="methodology.explain_cells_level_unexplained"/> and <DataFig path="methodology.explain_cells_unexplained"/>. The unexplained ones all involve public sector companies: the interest and dividends they pay to banks and NBFCs, and the interest and grants they receive. The map flags every one of them, and the flow panel shows each entry's value before and after balancing.

**Not every limit was moved by data.** <DataFig path="methodology.constraints_binding"/> constraints end exactly on the edge of their tolerance. Where that tolerance was a published figure's rounding or a cash-versus-accrual allowance, the edge is evidence. Where it was a judgement band, the edge is our choice: see [the limits](#limits).
