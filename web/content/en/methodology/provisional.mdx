Building the map needed some choices that no published source settles. The project owner approved the balanced SAM as the base for this site at its second checkpoint (Checkpoint B), and accepted the choices below **provisionally**, on the option we suggested; where that option is not yet in the data, the item says so. Each stays open: questions to the National Accounts Division of MoSPI are pending, and any of them can be revisited when it answers. The flows they touch are labelled estimated on the map (or set by balancing, where balancing set their level), with the method one click away.

- **Imputed pension contributions of government employees are not written** (D2-20). The evidence favours leaving them out; whether the national accounts include them is a question for MoSPI.
- **Factor income by household group uses the same fifth in an older national study** (D2-19): the rural and urban groups take the income gradient of the matching national group.
- **PM-KISAN is a cash transfer to farm families, not a production subsidy** (D2-24), because it pays a flat amount per family.
- **Part of personal income tax is paid by unincorporated firms** treated as companies (D2-11), following the household share in the previous national-accounts series.
- **The GST compensation cess used to repay the back-to-back loans is a current transfer** to the States (D2-13), not a capital transfer.
- **The previous series' structures fill flows the new series does not yet publish** (D2-21), until MoSPI publishes new sector accounts.
- **The States' motor-vehicle tax is a tax on products** (D2-4), not a direct tax on households.
- **The Centre and the States are separated as the national accounts separate them** (D2-8): money for central schemes released outside State treasuries counts as the States'.
- **The Union's income from the IMF and its SDR charges are recorded on the RBI** (D2-7). We suggested moving them to the Centre, and that suggestion was accepted, but the data have not been rebuilt since: on this site both still sit with the RBI. They are small next to GDP.
- **The tolerance bands that set levels** (see [limits](#limits)) keep their current widths.
- **Open findings and smaller choices are accepted as they stand in the data:**
  - the surplus that public sector companies keep, with no evidenced outlet (the moves it causes are labelled unexplained);
  - the split of saving between banks and NBFCs, which follows the flow of funds;
  - the household saving profile across the ten groups;
  - the consumption the survey does not capture, spread like the part it does (no top-heavy variant);
  - the banks' service margin (FISIM) on household loans, kept on the current method;
  - the key for who receives private companies' dividends and other distributed income, kept on the flow-of-funds holder shares;
  - the unexplained remainder in the bridge from government budgets to general-government saving, kept as a remainder;
  - a possible overlap between revenue grants to public sector companies and the "business" subsidies in the Finance Commission's data, left as it is;
  - insurance premiums and claims by payer, which the balancing cannot yet require to match;
  - the uneven band on households' fund-held interest from public sector company bonds;
  - a few further time series that could be ratio-linked to the new base, kept unlinked.
- **Two proposed adjustments are not yet in the data**: adding urban local bodies' capital spending to the key that splits government investment between the Centre and the States, and bounding the share of foreign direct investment income paid abroad by financial firms to the range in the RBI's census of foreign liabilities and assets. Both wait for the next rebuild; until then the flows they touch are as balanced.
