The project keeps a public log of everything that does not close (the ISSUES file in the repository). These open items affect what you see here:

- **Who receives property income is the weakest link.** Without new-base sector accounts, households' interest and dividend income is filled with old-base structures and then moved by balancing to reach the national total of household saving. The total is evidenced; the split is not.
- **The Centre and the States.** Balancing moved saving, government consumption and some tax receipts between the Centre and the States. The direction has support in the flow of funds; the levels are set by tolerance bands.
- **Public sector companies keep more than the national accounts say they save**, and the balancing had nowhere evidenced to put it. The resulting moves in the interest, dividends and grants they pay and receive are labelled unexplained.
- **The States' subsidies** come out well above what State budgets report, because of how the national-accounts subsidy total is defined. This is a question for MoSPI.
- **Financial firms' saving.** Two official sources disagree on how it is split between banks and NBFCs; the map follows the flow of funds.
- **The household saving profile.** Saving rates by group rest on survey keys and on an older study's income pattern, and the rural richest fifth looks implausibly low.
- **National accounts and the flow of funds disagree** on how much each sector lends or borrows. The national accounts are the control, and the differences sit in named residuals.
- **Old and new series do not line up evenly.** MoSPI expects to publish the new-base back series later; until then the time series shows the break.

**Reuse of sources.** This is a personal project, and no special permissions have been sought from the publishers. The site shows only figures computed by the model from published statistics, each credited to its publisher. It does not republish the publishers' own files or tables, and it does not link directly to RBI pages (ISSUES P1-9).

**Questions for MoSPI's National Accounts Division.** New-base sector accounts; how departmental enterprises' surplus and pensions are recorded; whether government income includes imputed pension contributions; the subsidy concept; how PM-KISAN, motor-vehicle tax, securities transaction tax and the cess-funded loan repayment are classified; depreciation of dwellings by sector; and the source of imputed rent. One answer could replace several judgement calls on this page.
