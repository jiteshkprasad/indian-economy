This site shows India's economy for one financial year, **2023-24**, as a single table of payments called a **social accounting matrix** (SAM). Every account in the economy appears twice: once as a **row**, where it receives money, and once as a **column**, where it pays money. So every cell answers one question: *how much did the column pay the row?* For every account, what comes in equals what goes out. That is what "balanced" means.

The SAM has <DataFig path="sam.accounts" fmt="count"/> accounts: industries and the products they sell, wages and profits, households in ten groups, companies, banks and other financial firms, the Union government (the Centre), the States, each kind of tax, the rest of the world, saving and investment, and financial instruments. It is built on India's new national-accounts series, whose base year is 2022-23, and uses the latest published figures for 2023-24.

India's GDP in 2023-24 was <DataFig path="gdp.value" fmt="inr"/> <SummaryBadge path="gdp"/>. Every "share of GDP" on this site divides by that figure.

### Why the flows add up to far more than GDP

GDP counts each final good or service once. The map shows **gross flows**: it also shows the inputs firms buy from each other, the money that passes through financial markets, and transfers such as taxes, pensions and remittances. A single rupee can appear in several flows as it moves: a household pays a shop, the shop pays its workers, the workers pay tax. Add up every cell and you get <DataFig path="sam.grand_total" fmt="inr"/>, several times GDP. Nothing is double counted; the money is simply followed at every step.

### What this is not

**This is not a forecast, and it is not a model of behaviour.** It records what was paid in a year that has ended. It does not predict what will happen next, and it does not say what would happen if a policy changed. Where a story says "of every ₹100", it means the average share in 2023-24, not what an extra rupee would do. Working out ripple effects needs a model with its own assumptions, which comes in a later part of this project.
